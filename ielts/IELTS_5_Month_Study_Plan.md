## Overview & Strategy

**5 months × 2–3 hrs/day = \~300–450 hours total prep.**

Time allocation:

- **Writing & Speaking: \~60% of daily time** — your gap skills. Every extra hour here pays off most.
- **Reading & Listening: \~40%** — Maintenance + weak sub-skill drilling. Never drop these; scores fall fast without practice.

Based on her review, her key techniques translate directly to your situation:

## Monthly Roadmap

### Month 1 — November 2026: Diagnose & Build Foundations

**Goal:** Understand exactly why Writing and Speaking are at 5.5. Map your error patterns before drilling.

- **Writing:** Read the Band Descriptors. Do 1 Task 2 + 1 Task 1 per week. For each, copy 2 sample essays and extract Key Vocab + Diamond Vocab (Thảo Lê's method). Prioritize Task Achievement and Coherence first — they weigh most.
- **Speaking:** Record yourself daily answering Part 1 and Part 2 prompts. Listen back; note where you stall, repeat, or lack vocabulary. Start building topical vocab for the 6 big IELTS themes: Government, Environment, Health, Technology, Work, Education.
- **Reading:** 1 full Cambridge practice test per week. Build keyword tables for every wrong answer.
- **Listening:** 3 Cambridge tests per week + dictation on 1 Part 3 per week (written by hand).

### Month 2 — December 2026: Structured Skill-Building

**Goal:** Establish a consistent writing process and Speaking fluency habits.

- **Writing:** 2 Task 2 + 1 Task 1 per week. Find a tutor or community (e.g. Han Ngo's Facebook group) for feedback. Focus on paragraphing logic: every body paragraph needs a claim, explanation, and example.
- **Speaking:** Find a speaking partner at your level or in higher. Aim for 3 mock Part 2+3 sessions per week. Begin using Mat Clark's Part 3 question-type framework.
- **Reading:** 2 practice tests per week. Drill your weakest question type for 20–30 min each session (use your error tracking table to identify it).
- **Listening:** Add 20–30 min/day of enjoyable English input — podcasts, YouTube, TikTok in native English.

### Month 3 — January 2027: Intensive Gap-Closing

**Goal:** Push Writing and Speaking to 6.0–6.5. The hardest and most critical month.

- **Writing:** 3 essays per week. Get feedback on every essay. Shift focus to Lexical Resource and Grammatical Range — the criteria that separate 6.0 from 7.0.
- **Speaking:** Record 3–4 times per week. Shadow native speakers from your listening input. Attention on pronunciation and intonation (these affect Fluency scores).
- **Reading + Listening:** Timed full tests only. Strict 60-min Reading, strict Listening timing. Track band by question type weekly.

### Month 4 — February 2027: Consolidation & Mock Tests

**Goal:** Simulate exam conditions. Find and close remaining gaps.

- Run 1 full 4-skill mock exam per week under real conditions.
- After each mock: analyze every wrong answer, weak paragraph, and Speaking disfluency.
- **Writing:** Review your best and worst essays from months 1–3. Build a personal bank of sentence structures and transitions that reliably work for you.
- **Speaking:** Drill Part 2 cue cards across all 5 topic groups (People, Objects, Places, Experiences, Others). Record and self-assess against the Band Descriptors.

### Month 5 — March 2027: Final Sprint & Confidence

**Goal:** Peak performance, reduce anxiety, hold all skills steady.

- 2 full mock exams per week.
- Light but consistent Writing (1 essay/week) — don't burn out in the last stretch.
- Heavy Speaking: daily Part 1 warm-ups, 3× Part 2+3 full mock per week.
- **Final week:** Stop new material. Review your personal vocab bank, best sample essays, and Speaking notes. Prioritize sleep and rest over extra study hours.

## Reading (7.0 → Maintain & Strengthen)

You're already at target. The goal is to hold this score with minimal time investment while sharpening your weakest question types.

### What to do

1. **Keyword tables** — After every practice test, build a table of keywords from wrong answers + their synonyms/antonyms as used in the passage. This trains you to spot paraphrasing traps — the same method Thảo Lë used to reach 9.0.
2. **Error tracking by question type** — Log your score and mistake type after each test. Once you know your weakest type (e.g. True/False/Not Given, Matching Headings), dedicate 20–30 min sessions drilling only that type rather than always doing full tests.
3. **Timed discipline** — Always practice under strict 60-minute pressure. Leave 5 min for transfer and checking.
4. **Frequency:** 2 tests/week in Months 1–3 → 1 test/week in Months 4–5.

### Materials

- Cambridge IELTS 10–18 (primary)
- Road to IELTS (British Council) for variety

## Listening (7.0 → Maintain & Strengthen)

Already at target. Focus on tightening Part 3 and Part 4 accuracy, and building immersive daily input to push consistency.

### What to do

1. **Keyword tables** — Same as Reading; use the transcripts at the back of Cambridge books to map what you heard vs. what the passage said.
2. **Dictation** — 1× Part 3 per week, written by hand. Pause and transcribe every word. Thảo Lê moved from 4.5 to 6.5 using this technique alone — at your level it will tighten accuracy on the harder parts.
3. **Note-taking during practice** — Especially for Part 2 (maps/plans) and Part 3 (matching, multiple choice). Write down key info rather than holding it in memory; this reduces cognitive load so you don't miss the next question while processing the last.
4. **Daily immersive input (20–30 min)** — Native English content you genuinely enjoy: podcasts, YouTube, TikTok. Thảo Lê attributes her jump from 7.5 to 8.5 to this habit. Enjoyment = consistency = the compounding effect.
5. **Frequency:** 3 Cambridge tests/week in Months 1–3 → timed full tests only from Month 4.

### Materials

- Cambridge IELTS 10–18
- BBC 6 Minute English, TED Talks, or any podcast you enjoy
- English TikTok / YouTube (native speakers, any topic)

## Writing (5.5 → 6.5+)

Your biggest gap skill. Needs the most structured, consistent attention across all 5 months.

### Why you're likely at 5.5

- Task 2: Underdeveloped body paragraphs — one idea with no clear explanation or example
- Task 1: Data described without a clear overview or grouped trend analysis
- Limited vocabulary range and repeated grammatical patterns
- Weak cohesion between sentences and paragraphs

### Step-by-step approach

**Step 1 — Understand the scoring criteria (Week 1)** Download the official IELTS Writing Band Descriptors (free on the IELTS website). Study what separates Band 5, 6, and 7 for each of the 4 criteria: Task Achievement, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy. Everything you write from now on should be evaluated against these four.

**Step 2 — Sample essay analysis (Thảo Lê's method)** For every sample essay you study, extract:

- **Key Vocab** — how did the author paraphrase the question's keywords?
- **Diamond Vocab** — advanced expressions, strong collocations, precise or unusual words
- **Connectives** — how sentences and paragraphs link to each other
- **Paragraph logic** — for Task 1: how is data grouped? For Task 2: what is the function of each sentence (claim / explanation / example / contrast)?

**Step 3 — Write, get feedback, rewrite**

- Target: 2 Task 2 + 1 Task 1 per week
- Get human feedback wherever possible: a tutor, Han Ngo's IELTS group on Facebook, or a study partner with a higher band
- After feedback: **always rewrite the essay** incorporating corrections. Reading feedback without rewriting barely helps.

**Step 4 — Build a personal phrase bank** Keep a running list of useful sentence openers, linking phrases, and structures that you can reliably use. Don't just collect them — use 2–3 from the bank in every essay you write.

### Materials

- Official IELTS Writing Band Descriptors (free download)
- Real IELTS Writing exam questions (widely shared online by year)
- IELTS Writing & Reading Group by Han Ngo (Facebook)
- Cambridge IELTS 10–18 (Writing tasks in each test)

## Speaking (5.5 → 6.5+)

Needs daily practice. Unlike Writing, Speaking improvements compound fastest when you practice little-and-often rather than in long blocks.

### Why you're likely at 5.5

- Long pauses or hesitation under pressure (hurts Fluency score)
- Short, underdeveloped Part 2 answers — can't comfortably fill 2 minutes
- Limited vocabulary for Part 3 abstract topics
- Part 3 answers lack argument structure — they trail off or repeat the same point

### Part 1 — Short, fluent, natural

- Aim for 2–3 sentence answers with 1 concrete reason or example
- Record yourself, listen back, and note where you stumble or repeat words
- Jimmy English Home on YouTube is highly recommended (Thảo Lê's pick) for topical vocabulary and natural Part 1 patterns

### Part 2 — Fill the full 2 minutes

- Group cue cards into 5 topic types: **People, Objects, Places, Experiences, Others** — prepare 1–2 cards per group each week
- Focus on the **Why** — personal feelings, specific memories, and reactions make answers sound natural and are easier to expand on
- Build a small bank of real personal stories that can be adapted across multiple prompts

### Part 3 — Treat it as spoken Writing Task 2

Use Mat Clark's question-type framework to identify what structure to use:

- Different kinds of... → categorize + give examples
- Comparing (time / objects / concepts) → then vs. now, or A vs. B
- Predicting → "It's likely that... because..."
- Advantages and Disadvantages → balanced view
- Problems and Solutions → name the problem, give a solution + reason
- Opinion (Why) → claim → explain → example → optional contrast

For each answer type, have a go-to phrase opener ready so you buy thinking time without pausing.

### Finding a speaking partner

This is what moved Thảo Lê's score most. Options:

- IELTS Facebook groups (Vietnam-based ones are very active — post looking for a partner at Band 5.5–6.5)
- iTalki for paid sessions with an IELTS-experienced tutor (even 1× per week is high-value)
- HelloTalk / Tandem apps for free exchange partners
- Target: 3 mock Part 2+3 sessions per week, with feedback after each

### Input for output

20–30 min/day of English you enjoy — podcasts, TikTok, TV series. This builds the vocabulary and phrasing that comes out naturally when you speak. Thảo Lê answered a live exam question using an idea she'd heard on TikTok the week before.
